# Stable layouts and niche types open Linework's lane

As of November 2026 the architecture-diagram market has four layers, and none of them covers the space Linework is aiming at. First, ubiquitous text renderers (Mermaid, PlantUML, Graphviz, mingrammer Diagrams) win on distribution but are criticised for spaghetti layouts. Second, a handful of better-looking text tools (D2/TALA, LikeC4, Structurizr, Ilograph) lack native rendering where docs live. Third, free or seat-priced canvases (draw.io, Excalidraw, Lucidchart, Miro, IcePanel, Eraser) look polished but drift from reality and diff badly. Fourth, cloud and IaC auto-diagrammers (Cloudcraft, Hava, Lucidscale, KubeDiagrams, TerraVision) produce output that is accurate but rarely presentation-ready. Users want two things at once. They want text in git that an LLM can write, and they want layouts that don't move when they add an edge, with a look they can show a client. The loudest complaints across every text tool are uncontrolled layout and unstable re-layout; across canvases the loudest is staleness. Linework's design (YAML source, sidecar pins, deterministic dot placement plus its own orthogonal router and scored candidates, one modern theme, slide export) answers both complaint categories, and its specialist types land in niches with no as-code competitor at all: data-mesh product maps, access diagrams, developer journeys and branching strategies. It is still missing the distribution plumbing that decided this market: an MCP server, a GitHub Action, and importers that keep pins on re-import. Those, not more diagram types, are the "now" work. Two 2026 events make the timing good. Terrastruct shut down and open-sourced TALA, which leaves D2 without a commercial steward. Structurizr's cloud service also reached end of life.

## Four market layers, and 2026 shook three of them

The text-first layer is dominated by **Mermaid (91k GitHub stars, MIT)**, whose advantage is distribution, not quality. GitHub has rendered it natively in Markdown since February 2022 ([GitHub blog](https://github.blog/2022-02-14-include-diagrams-markdown-files-mermaid)), and practitioners put it bluntly: D2 "is better, but it's not supported by GitHub. Go where your users are" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45713312)). Mermaid's company, rebranded mermaid.ai, sells hosted editing and AI credits at $10–20/user/month, billed annually ([mermaid.ai/pricing](https://mermaid.ai/pricing)). PlantUML (13.4k stars, LGPL) gets native rendering in GitLab ([GitLab docs](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/markdown/)) but feels "a bit stale". mingrammer Diagrams (42.8k stars) has open issues, nearly all from 2020, asking for arrows to and from clusters (+121) and control over node positions (+28) ([#17](https://github.com/mingrammer/diagrams/issues/17), [#65](https://github.com/mingrammer/diagrams/issues/65)).

The model-and-views layer moved most in 2026. **Terrastruct shut down on 5 September 2026**. D2 became a non-profit project fiscally sponsored by Hack Club ([D2 blog](https://d2lang.com/blog/d2-non-profit/)), and two days later the formerly paid TALA layout engine went **MPL-2.0 open source** in D2 v0.9.0 ([D2 blog](https://d2lang.com/blog/tala-is-open-source/)). The maintainer also announced that "the era" of handcrafted code is over and development will lean on AI, and HN commenters questioned the sponsorship model ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49589768)). **Structurizr's cloud service hit end of life on 30 September 2026**, and Lite, the CLI and on-prem were folded into one tool ([Structurizr EOL](https://docs.structurizr.com/eol)). **LikeC4 (6k stars, MIT, TypeScript)** is the most active structural competitor. It offers custom element kinds, a VS Code live preview, React components, an MCP server, and a `validate` command that fails CI on "layout drift (outdated manual layout)" ([likec4.dev](https://likec4.dev/), [CLI docs](https://likec4-docs-astro.pages.dev/tooling/cli)).

The canvas layer splits into free tools (draw.io, Excalidraw at 135k stars) and seat-priced SaaS: Lucidchart around $9–10/user/month, Miro $8–20/member/month, and IcePanel around $50–77/month per aggregators ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/diagramming/lucidchart/), [toolradar](https://toolradar.com/compare/miro-vs-excalidraw), [toolradar IcePanel](https://toolradar.com/tools/icepanel/pricing)). Eraser's pricing page lists Business at $45/member/month annually, with GitHub integration on paid plans only ([eraser.io/pricing](https://www.eraser.io/pricing)); an aggregator cites a $10 Pro tier, so the figures conflict. One licence change matters for anyone building an editor: **tldraw left MIT in SDK 4.0** and now needs a commercial licence for production use ([tldraw.dev/pricing](https://tldraw.dev/pricing)).

The auto-diagramming layer is where lock-in shows. **Cloudcraft is now "free in Datadog"**, meaning it comes with a Datadog subscription ([cloudcraft.co/pricing](https://cloudcraft.co/pricing)). Hava starts at roughly $44–59/month and disables exports during its trial ([techyorker](https://techyorker.com/products/hava/)). Brainboard charges per imported resource ([brainboard.co/pricing](https://brainboard.co/pricing)). The open generators (TerraVision, Rover, Inkdrop, AzViz, Microsoft's own azure-resource-visualizer skill that emits Mermaid) are free but draw dependency graphs, not documents ([TerraVision](https://github.com/patrickchugh/terravision), [azure-resource-visualizer](https://azure.github.io/git-ape/docs/skills/azure-resource-visualizer)).

Cutting across all four layers is **the 2026 MCP wave**. The official draw.io MCP server, created in February 2026, has **5,799 stars**, and the official Excalidraw MCP App has **5,656**. Each is around 60% of draw.io's main repo in nine months, and the Excalidraw one is listed in Claude's connector directory ([jgraph/drawio-mcp](https://github.com/jgraph/drawio-mcp), [Claude connector listing](https://claude.com/connectors/excalidraw-app-demo)). draw.io's own AI "Generate" feature runs on Claude and returns editable XML ([drawio.com](https://www.drawio.com/docs/manual/generate/ai-models/)).

| Layer | Leaders | What wins them users | Main hate | Linework relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Text renderers | Mermaid, PlantUML, Graphviz, mingrammer | Native GitHub/GitLab rendering, LLM fluency, free | Spaghetti layout, reshuffles, ugly, headless Chrome/Java | Beat on layout and looks; can't beat on native rendering |
| Model + views | D2/TALA, LikeC4, Structurizr, Ilograph | Pinning, orthogonal layout, one model many views | No native rendering, steward risk (D2), C4 rigidity | Closest competitors; LikeC4 is the one to watch |
| Canvases | draw.io, Excalidraw, Lucid, Miro, IcePanel, Eraser | Free/offline (draw.io), polish, collaboration | Staleness, alignment drudgery, binary diffs, seats | Linework's builder must match canvas ergonomics |
| Auto-diagrammers | Cloudcraft, Hava, Lucidscale, KubeDiagrams, TerraVision | Live sync with cloud/IaC | Not presentation-ready, paid, vendor lock-in | Importers + pins = sync and polish together |

## Users love text in git and hate layouts that fight back

The love side is consistent. "Having it versioned as text is the big win" is the most common reason people pick diagrams-as-code ([HN 43343473](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43343473)). draw.io is loved for being "free forever, no account, no limits, offline" ([softwaremany](https://softwaremany.pages.dev/blog/2025-10-20-diagramming-tools-comparison/)), and TALA's locked-coordinate hybrid placement is its best-liked feature ([D2 positions](https://d2lang.com/tour/positions/)). Users split on how they want to steer layout. Some want to "lock the coordinates for certain components" and let the engine do the rest; others want weights instead of x/y; others want to generate with AI and then adjust visually, because "LLM refinement of layouts [is] hard, interactive editing much faster" ([HN 45706792](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45706792)). Linework's pin-in-sidecar model plus builder serves all three groups.

The hate side has a clear order. **Staleness is the number-one problem** in IcePanel's 2025 survey of architects. "Keeping documentation up to date" leads, followed by missing standards, choosing the level of detail, and time pressure ([IcePanel 2025](https://icepanel.io/blog/state-of-software-architecture-survey-2025)). The sample is small (n=75), vendor-run and C4-leaning, so treat it as directional. **Layout control is the dominant complaint about text tools.** A PlantUML user spends "20-30% of my time desperately trying to tweak the layout with hints" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45706792)). PlantUML issue #13, which calls the diagrams "a mess… spaghetti", has been open since 2016 ([plantuml#13](https://github.com/plantuml/plantuml/issues/13)). For Mermaid, "a single new connection can blow up the whole layout" ([HN 43343473](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43343473)). Graphviz output "jumps around a lot… snapshot to snapshot" ([HN 45707539](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45707539)). Even TALA's author admits that adding one node can make a diagram "look completely different" because of random seeds ([D2 blog](https://d2lang.com/blog/tala-is-open-source/)). **Ports and edge bundling are the specific mechanics people ask for**: PlantUML port placement (+28), D2 "specify ports" (+14), D2's lack of hyperedges, and Mermaid routing that "tends to blow up" with many lines into one node ([plantuml#1766](https://github.com/plantuml/plantuml/issues/1766), [d2#628](https://github.com/d2lang/d2/issues/628), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49589768)). **Looks block adoption beyond engineers.** D2's default theme is "a hard sell to orgs where management isn't technical" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45707539)), Graphviz is seen as too amateurish for slides, and Excalidraw-style sketches "probably won't [work] in a presentation or for official documentation" ([Pluralsight](https://www.pluralsight.com/resources/blog/cloud/the-top-cloud-diagramming-tools-ranked)). **Heavy tooling annoys people**: Mermaid's CLI "goes and runs Chrome, which feels insane" ([HN 43343473](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43343473)).

One trend affects distribution strategy. Collaborative wikis as the architecture source of truth rose **from 29% to 42%**, while dedicated tools fell **from 37% to 24%** ([IcePanel 2025](https://icepanel.io/blog/state-of-software-architecture-survey-2025)). Architects want diagrams embedded where docs already live, not in another destination app. That suits an offline tool that exports clean SVG, as long as getting that SVG into GitHub, Confluence and MkDocs is effortless.

## Specialist niches range from wide open to saturated

The niches differ sharply in how much competition they have. The evidence is also uneven. Demand for the data-mesh, IAM and journey niches is **inferred from missing supply, not from documented user requests**, so validate it before betting heavily.

| Niche | Incumbents | The gap | Linework status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data products / contracts | Entropy Data (Team from €20k/yr), Collibra, OpenMetadata, Atlan; datacontract-cli exports Mermaid erDiagram only | No as-code tool draws a data-mesh map (domains, product types, ports, contracts as edges) | Built (overview + detail, ODCS v2/v3 import); ODPS import missing |
| IAM / roles | BloodHound CE (attack paths), AzGovViz, PIMSCAN (HTML reports) | No documentation-grade "who can do what where" diagram from Entra/Azure/GitHub exports | Built; importers missing |
| Developer journeys | TheyDo, Smaply, UXPressia (seat-priced CX SaaS); Mermaid `journey` | Nothing specific to developer experience, nothing linked to architecture | Planned |
| Git branching | Mermaid gitGraph; gitgraph.js archived 2024 | No branching-strategy view (lanes, environments, release trains) | Planned |
| Kubernetes / network | KubeDiagrams, k8sviz, netbox-topology-views, PlantUML nwdiag | Dated icon look, every resource drawn, no abstraction levels; Mermaid network topology request +329 | Planned |
| ERD | dbdiagram (DBML), ChartDB (23k stars), Azimutt, DrawSQL, DBeaver | Crowded; only layout quality at scale is unsolved | Built; DDL/DBML/Prisma import missing |

**Data products are the clearest gap.** The standards have settled: ODCS v3.1.0 (December 2025) added `relationships` and deprecated the top-level `dataProduct` field in favour of ODPS. ODPS v1.0.0 shipped in September 2025, and both are YAML on JSON Schema ([Bitol ODCS](https://bitol.io/bitol-announces-odcs-v3-1-0-stronger-smarter-and-stricter/), [Bitol ODPS](https://bitol.io/announcing-odps-v1-0-0-building-the-language-of-data-products/)). Yet the only as-code rendering is datacontract-cli's Mermaid erDiagram, which shows schema only ([datacontract docs](https://docs.datacontract.com/docs/exports/mermaid.md)). Platforms draw ports as generic lineage graphs ([OpenMetadata](https://docs.open-metadata.org/latest/how-to-guides/data-governance/domains-&-data-products/data-products), [Collibra](https://productresources.collibra.com/docs/collibra/2026.02/Content/Assets/DataProducts/ta_conf-data-product.htm)), and Entropy Data's paid tiers start at €20,000/year ([entropy-data.com](https://www.entropy-data.com/)). Linework already has ODCS import. Adding **ODPS import (products and ports) and v3.1 `relationships` in the detail view** turns this into a category of one.

**IAM is a split market.** Security graphs such as BloodHound CE (with AzureHound JSON as the de facto Entra export) target attack paths ([SpecterOps](https://specterops.io/blog/2025/07/29/bloodhound-community-edition-v8-launches-with-opengraph/)). Governance tools such as AzGovViz produce reports, not diagrams ([AzGovViz](https://github.com/azure/azure-governance-visualizer)). A diagram that shows the scope tree, principals, and role assignments coloured by role (dashed when PIM-eligible), with an access-matrix rendering of the same model, fits audit and documentation work that neither group serves.

**Git flow and K8s have weak incumbents.** gitgraph.js was archived on 13 July 2024, and its maintainer points to Mermaid ([gitgraph.js](https://github.com/nicoespeon/gitgraph.js)). Mermaid gitGraph orders commits strictly by source and still has open requests for squash and custom icons ([Mermaid weekly report](https://buttondown.com/weekly-project-news/archive/weekly-github-report-for-mermaid-january-16-2026)). KubeDiagrams proves the demand: it reads manifests, Kustomize, Helm (including OCI) and live cluster state, and has an arXiv paper ([arXiv 2505.22879](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22879v1)). But it renders in the classic Graphviz-icon style. **The Mermaid network topology request, open since 2020, has +329 votes** ([Mermaid issues](https://github.com/mermaid-js/mermaid/issues/4628)), the strongest documented demand signal for any of Linework's planned types.

**ERD is saturated, but its leader has a known layout problem.** dbdiagram users report auto-arrange that packs tables too tightly, bunches them to the left and runs lines under tables. The team calls it "already on our backlog", with no fix shipped ([dbdiagram community](https://community.dbdiagram.io/t/improvements-to-auto-arrange-feature/2551)). Azimutt is the only tool that handles about 1,000 tables, and it does so by starting from an empty view and exploring outward ([Azimutt docs](https://azimutt.pubstack.io/docs/layouts)). Prisma users render ERDs through Mermaid and puppeteer ([prisma-erd-generator](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/prisma-erd-generator@2.4.4/README.md)). Linework wins ERD only with better layout at scale, a focus-plus-N-hops subset view, and DBML/DDL/Prisma import that lets dbdiagram and Prisma users move over.

## Linework beats the field on stability, not just beauty

**Make "adding an edge doesn't reshuffle the diagram" the headline, and prove it.** No competitor optimises for stability. Dot jumps between snapshots, TALA is seed-random by its own admission, and Mermaid blows up on one new connection. Linework already picks among candidate layouts by a bends/crossings score. Adding a **displacement-from-previous-layout term**, and keeping the previous winner unless a new candidate is clearly better, gives it a property no TALA, dot or ELK user gets today ([D2 blog](https://d2lang.com/blog/tala-is-open-source/)). Research metrics also suggest score terms for area or aspect ratio, minimum clearance and edge-length variance, which match the Hegemann–Wolff evaluation set ([arXiv 2309.01671](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.01671v2)). With no public architecture-diagram benchmark in existence, publishing one (sample diagrams scored against Mermaid, D2/TALA and PlantUML) is cheap credibility.

**Close the routing gap with a post-routing stage, not a new router.** Linework's A* with bend and length weights is roughly where Excalidraw's elbow arrows are ([Excalidraw blog](https://plus.excalidraw.com/blog/building-elbow-arrows-part-two)). libavoid, yFiles and MSAGL are ahead because of what happens after routing: they order edges within shared channels, then **nudge** segments apart into free space. That one stage also solves even port spreading, because port positions fall out of the same separation solve ([libavoid docs](https://www.adaptagrams.org/documentation/libavoid.html), [arXiv 2309.01671](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.01671v2)). For trunks, the cheap proven pattern is ELK's "Merge Edges" or libavoid's "hyperedges from common source": route one edge, then join the siblings at a junction ([ELK libavoid reference](https://eclipse.dev/elk/reference/algorithms/org-eclipse-elk-alg-libavoid.html)). **MSAGL.js is MIT, pure TypeScript and ships a rectilinear router with nudging plus IPSep-CoLa constraint layout** ([microsoft/msagljs](https://github.com/microsoft/msagljs)). It can be depended on or ported without licence friction. libavoid and HOLA, by contrast, are LGPL, and TALA is MPL-2.0 file-level copyleft. IPSep-CoLa is also the right tool for honouring pins without leaving the rest of the graph to dot. On automatic direction choice, no engine (ELK included) documents automatic direction and no 2022–2026 paper addresses it, so Linework can simply add direction as another candidate dimension in its existing scorer.

**Turn the sidecar into a sync story.** The sidecar `.layout.json` is a real differentiator: D2 pins pollute the source, and oxdraw stores them as Mermaid comments ([HN 45706792](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45706792)). Copy LikeC4's drift check: `linework validate` should fail when pins reference deleted nodes or the model has diverged ([LikeC4 CLI](https://likec4-docs-astro.pages.dev/tooling/cli)). Then pair the sidecar with importers. No surveyed tool does **diff-preserving re-import into a human-editable text file**. Generators regenerate from scratch, and canvases never re-sync. "Import from Bicep/Terraform/Helm/ODPS, hand-tune, re-import next sprint with your pins intact" answers the number-one pain (staleness) and the number-two pain (layout fiddling) together.

**Treat the builder's missing basics as table stakes.** Visual editors are already criticised as "too fiddly" ([HN 43343473](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43343473)). A builder without multi-select, copy-paste and align/distribute will read as a toy to anyone coming from draw.io or Excalidraw. This is cheap relative to its impact on first impressions.

**Use the agent wave instead of fighting it.** Typed YAML with a validator suits LLMs well: an agent writes YAML, gets schema errors back and iterates. Generic Mermaid is weak here, since LLMs "don't understand the C4 model and don't respect its rules" ([Patreon, Simon Brown](https://www.patreon.com/posts/152027514)), and IBM's MermaidSeqBench found "significant capability gaps across models" ([arXiv 2511.14967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14967)). LikeC4 and Structurizr already ship MCP servers ([docs.structurizr.com/ai](https://docs.structurizr.com/ai)). For Linework, an MCP server that exposes validate, render and the JSON Schema is the minimum. An MCP App that renders inline in chat follows the draw.io and Excalidraw pattern that drew about 5.7k stars each.

**Win the consultant deck, an uncontested slot.** IcePanel Flows is the only standout storytelling feature, and no surveyed tool is known for exporting a ready 16:9 slide with consistent styling ([IcePanel](https://icepanel.io/blog/2025-11-14-icepanel-vs-structurizr)). Linework already has 16:9 export. Adding a step-through overlay (highlight path 1, then 2) serves git flow, developer journeys and client walkthroughs together. Brand tokens (colours, font, logo) on top of the single theme address the consultant need without giving up the consistent look. The evidence for consultant branding needs is thin, though, and interviews would firm it up.

**Handle cloud icons carefully.** Microsoft permits Azure icons "in architectural diagrams, training materials, or documentation" and forbids distorting Entra icons. Redistribution terms have been disputed on GitHub ([Azure icons](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/icons/), [issue #2251](https://www.github.com/MicrosoftDocs/architecture-center/issues/2251)). The safe path for an MIT repo is to fetch the official pack at install time or have users supply it, and never recolour the icons.

## Roadmap: distribution first, importers second, model last

| Horizon | Item | Rationale tied to evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | GitHub Action: render on push, commit SVG, post before/after images on PRs | Native rendering decided this market; Mermaid wins on it despite worse layout. A visual-diff PR comment is something no competitor offers |
| **Now** | MCP server (validate, render, schema, examples) + published JSON Schema + llms.txt | Official draw.io/Excalidraw MCP servers reached ~5.7k stars in 9 months; LikeC4 and Structurizr ship MCP; typed YAML plus errors suits agents |
| **Now** | Builder basics: multi-select, copy-paste, align/distribute | Canvases set the ergonomic bar; "fiddly" is the complaint to avoid |
| **Now** | Stability term in the candidate score + `validate` drift check for pins | Reshuffle-on-edit is the most-cited complaint across Mermaid, Graphviz and TALA; LikeC4's drift check is proven |
| **Now** | ODPS import + ODCS v3.1 `relationships` in the detail view | Category of one; standards are stable YAML; the only competitor output is a Mermaid erDiagram |
| **Next** | Channel ordering + nudging stage (even port spread falls out), then trunks via shared-source junctions | Ports and bundling are open issues in PlantUML, D2, mingrammer and Mermaid; MSAGL.js (MIT) gives a licence-clean reference |
| **Next** | Auto direction as a candidate dimension in the scorer | No engine does it; cheap given the existing scorer |
| **Next** | Kubernetes type + importer (`helm template`, `kustomize build`, `kubectl -o yaml`) with app-level vs full-resource views | KubeDiagrams proves demand; its dated, draw-everything output is the gap |
| **Next** | Networking type (nwdiag-style buses) + NetBox import | Mermaid network topology request +329, open since 2020 |
| **Next** | ERD importers: SQL DDL (Postgres/MySQL), DBML, Prisma; focus + N-hops subset view | dbdiagram auto-arrange complaints unresolved; Prisma users stuck on Mermaid layout; Azimutt shows subset views scale |
| **Next** | Access importers: `az role assignment list`, AzureHound JSON, GitHub REST teams/repos | Turns the built roles type into a docs/audit tool nobody else offers |
| **Later** | Shared element catalogue across types, cross-diagram links, drill-down | Most-requested model feature (Structurizr, D2 #711 top issue, LikeC4), but a large architectural change |
| **Later** | Git flow strategy mode (lanes, environments, release trains) + optional `git log` import | Weak incumbent (Mermaid gitGraph); low documented demand |
| **Later** | Developer journey type linking steps to architecture nodes + step-through overlay | Unique once the shared catalogue exists; demand is inferred, so it should wait on the catalogue |
| **Later** | Bicep/ARM/Terraform plan import for Azure, keeping pins | Highest staleness payoff, but mature competitors (Hava, TerraVision, Microsoft's skill) already exist |
| **Later** | Embeddable renderer package, VS Code preview, MkDocs/Docusaurus plugins, Kroki-compatible endpoint, Mermaid/draw.io export | Embeds drove Excalidraw/Mermaid growth; wiki-as-source-of-truth rose to 42%; draw.io interop is repeatedly requested (D2 #743, Kroki #405) |

The ordering follows one principle. The market punished better-quality tools that were hard to get into existing workflows (D2, Structurizr cloud), so Linework ships the plumbing that removes that friction before it adds breadth. The importers come next, ranked by how open each niche is and how cheap the source format is to parse: ODPS and K8s are YAML, and DDL and DBML are well specified. The shared model comes last because it is the biggest change and LikeC4 and Structurizr already own the "model plus views" story.

## Positioning: diagrams that hold still, for the domains nobody draws well

**One-line positioning:** "Architecture diagrams as YAML that look presentation-ready and don't move when you change them, with first-class types for data products, access and cloud." This picks fights Linework can win (stability, looks, niche semantics) and avoids the one it can't (native GitHub rendering).

**Lead with a wedge, not the full menu.** The data-mesh map is the strongest launch story: an empty as-code niche, a standards-literate YAML audience, and commercial alternatives priced at €20k+/year. Lead the launch with "render your ODPS/ODCS files as a data-mesh map in one command", then broaden to Azure and access diagrams for the same consultants and platform teams. Data-community channels (Bitol/ODCS community, datacontract-cli users) are a natural first audience. Asking to be listed as an ODCS/ODPS visualiser in their tooling docs costs nothing.

**Ride the D2 and Structurizr disruption carefully.** D2 users who wanted polished text-first architecture diagrams now face a project without a commercial steward. Structurizr cloud users were forced to self-host. A short "coming from D2/Structurizr" guide, and eventually an importer, captures that intent. Don't over-claim: TALA is now free and good, and LikeC4 is MIT and pushes code almost daily. The honest differentiators are deterministic stable layout, sidecar pins, typed domain diagrams and the slide export.

**Show, don't claim.** The launch artefact should be a side-by-side benchmark page: the same five diagrams in Mermaid, D2/TALA, PlantUML and Linework, scored on crossings, bends and displacement after one added edge. Show HN posts with this shape do well (oxdraw got 266 points ([HN 45706792](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45706792))), and Thoughtworks Radar coverage gave D2 credibility with architects ([Radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-gb/radar/tools/d2)).

**Stay pure MIT and offline.** The permissive winners grew by being embeddable (Excalidraw at 135k stars, Mermaid at 91k), while closed layout engines did not sustain Terrastruct and tldraw's licence enforcement drew backlash ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/9/tldraw-sdk-4-0-adds-five-new-starter-kits-cli-tool-and-enforces-licensing-changes)). If Linework ever monetises, the evidence points to hosted collaboration or AI services, which is the mermaid.ai model, not to gating the router. The no-headless-browser TypeScript CLI is worth marketing explicitly against Mermaid's Chrome dependency.

## Conclusion

Diagramming tools have mostly competed on how much they can draw. Linework's strongest bet is how little its diagrams change. Stability, pins that survive re-import, and drift checks in CI make a diagram something a team can own the way it owns code, and that answers staleness, the complaint no amount of AI generation has fixed. The niche types give that bet a specific audience to start with.

The main risk is demand, not technology: the data-mesh, IAM and journey gaps are documented by missing supply, not by users asking for them. Before investing beyond the "now" column, ship the ODPS map and the GitHub Action, put them in front of data-mesh and Azure-consulting users, and let adoption, not the gap analysis, decide whether journeys and git flow ever leave "later".
