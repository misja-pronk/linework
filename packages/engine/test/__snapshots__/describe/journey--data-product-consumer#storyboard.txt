Finding and using a data product

Journey map, storyboard: Storyboard. Mei Tanaka's journey through 5 phases and 9 steps.

Persona: Mei Tanaka
- Role: Marketing analyst, consumer of data products
- Goal: Answer the churn question before the quarterly review
- Says: “I do not need all the data. I need data I can trust by Thursday.”

By phase
- Find
  - Searches the catalogue for customer data (feels hopeful, 3 of 5)
    - Thinks: “Five results called customer. Which one is the real one?”
    - Through Data catalogue
    - Pain: Duplicates and old tables in the results
    - Idea: Mark certified products in search
  - Asks a colleague which product to use (feels unsure, 2 of 5)
    - Says: “Use Customer 360, the others are leftovers.”
    - Through Teams chat
    - With Senior analyst
    - Pain: The right product is tribal knowledge
    - Measured: Half a day
- Understand
  - Reads the product page and the data contract (feels reassured, 4 of 5)
    - Says: “Owner, refresh time and quality checks, all on one page.”
    - Through Customer 360 product page and Data contract
    - Works well: Clear owner and service levels
    - Works well: Sample data without a request
  - Checks freshness and quality scores (feels confident, 4 of 5)
    - Thinks: “Refreshed at six this morning. Good enough.”
    - Through Quality dashboard
    - Works well: Quality is measured, not promised
    - Measured: 99.2% complete
- Get access
  - Requests access to the output port (feels impatient, 2 of 5)
    - Thinks: “Why do I have to explain the purpose three times?”
    - Through Access portal and Email
    - With Data product owner
    - Pain: Purpose is asked in three forms
    - Pain: No status after submitting
    - Idea: One request with the purpose attached to the contract
    - Measured: 4 days waiting
  - Gets approval and a connection string (feels relieved, 3 of 5)
    - Says: “Finally. Now where do I paste this?”
    - Through Email
    - With Data product owner
    - Pain: Connection details arrive by email
- Use
  - Queries the product from a notebook (feels productive, 5 of 5)
    - Says: “The join keys just work.”
    - Through Notebook and SQL endpoint
    - Works well: Stable schema with clear keys
    - Works well: Examples in the product page
    - Measured: First query in 20 min
  - Combines it with campaign data (feels puzzled, 3 of 5)
    - Thinks: “Customer id here, account id there.”
    - Through Notebook and Campaign product
    - Pain: Identifiers differ across domains
    - Idea: Shared customer key in every contract
- Give feedback
  - Shares the result and rates the product (feels heard, 4 of 5)
    - Says: “I left a note about the missing key. The owner replied the same day.”
    - Through Dashboard and Data catalogue
    - With Marketing lead
    - Works well: Feedback reaches the owning team

Feeling
Lowest at “Asks a colleague which product to use” (2 of 5); highest at “Queries the product from a notebook” (5 of 5).

Metrics
- Question to first query: 6 days
- Of which waiting: 4 days
- Products looked at: 5
