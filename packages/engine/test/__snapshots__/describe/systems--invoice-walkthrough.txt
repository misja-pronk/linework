Checkout without waiting

Architecture diagram: Proposal for the steering group. 9 elements in 2 groups, with 10 connections.

Groups and elements
- Customers (Users)
- Web shop (System)
  - Storefront (Web app)
  - Orders (Service)
  - Orders DB (Database)
  - New (Zone)
    - order-events (Queue)
    - Invoicing (Worker)
    - Notifier (Worker)
- ERP (Finance)
- Mail provider (Email)

Connections
- Customers connect to Storefront over HTTPS.
- Storefront calls Orders (REST).
- Orders sends data to Orders DB (SQL), calls ERP (create invoice), calls Mail provider (confirmation) and sends OrderPlaced to order-events.
- order-events sends messages to Invoicing and sends messages to Notifier.
- Invoicing calls ERP (create invoice).
- Notifier calls Mail provider (confirmation).

Walkthrough
1. Checkout today: A customer orders, and the Orders service does everything before it answers.
2. The order itself is quick: Storing the order takes 40 ms. This part works and stays as it is.
3. Then checkout waits for Finance: The invoice is created in the ERP while the customer looks at a spinner.
  - ERP: Maintenance 02:00 to 04:00: checkout fails
4. And for the mail provider: A slow confirmation mail is a slow checkout. Two outside systems decide how fast the shop feels.
5. Proposal: say what happened, and answer: Orders publishes OrderPlaced and answers the customer right away.
6. Invoices and mail follow on their own: Two small workers pick up the event. When the ERP is down they wait and try again; no order is lost.
  - order-events: Kept for 7 days
7. What the customer notices: Checkout answers in under 100 ms, also at night. Nothing changes in the storefront or the order data.

Key

- Solid blue line: Request
- Dashed amber line: Async message
