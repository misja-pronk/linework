Hub and spoke

Azure infrastructure diagram: Northwind · all traffic between on-premises, the spokes and the internet passes the hub firewall. 7 elements in 12 groups, with 6 connections.

Connections
- Edge router connects to vpng-hub (Private network).
- vpng-hub connects to afw-hub (Private network: UDR).
- afw-hub connects to vmss-erp (Private network: peering), connects to vmss-intranet (Private network: peering) and sends traffic to Internet (SNAT).
- bas-hub connects to vmss-erp (Management: RDP).

Walkthrough
1. The hub holds the shared network services; each workload gets its own spoke.
2. From the office: The office reaches the spokes over the VPN; route table rt-gateway sends it to the firewall, not the peering.
  - vpng-hub: rt-gateway: spokes → 10.0.0.68
3. Out to the internet: Each spoke subnet's route table (rt-erp, rt-intranet) sends 0.0.0.0/0 to the firewall at 10.0.0.68.
  - vmss-erp: rt-erp: 0.0.0.0/0 → 10.0.0.68
4. Spoke to spoke: Spokes are not peered to each other. ERP and intranet only meet through the firewall rules.
5. Admin access: No public IPs on VMs. Admins come in through Bastion; NSG nsg-app lets RDP in from AzureBastionSubnet only.
  - vmss-erp: nsg-app: RDP from AzureBastionSubnet only

Layers
Ways to look at this drawing by tag.
- Routing: Route tables rt-erp and rt-intranet send 0.0.0.0/0 to the firewall; rt-gateway sends the spoke ranges there too. (tags routing; 6 elements).
- Security: One way out to the internet, admin access only through Bastion. NSGs nsg-app and nsg-web guard the spoke subnets. (tags security; 4 elements).
