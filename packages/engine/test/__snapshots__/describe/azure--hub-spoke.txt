Hub and spoke

Azure infrastructure diagram: Northwind · traffic between on-premises, the spokes and the internet passes the hub firewall; admins use Bastion. 7 elements in 12 groups, with 6 connections.

Connections
- Edge router connects to vpng-hub (Private network).
- vpng-hub connects to afw-hub (Private network: UDR).
- afw-hub connects to vmss-erp-prod (Private network: peering), connects to vmss-intranet-prod (Private network: peering) and sends traffic to Internet (SNAT).
- bas-hub connects to vmss-erp-prod (Management: RDP).

Walkthrough
1. Hub and spokes: The hub holds the shared network services; each workload gets a spoke of its own.
2. From the office: rt-gateway-weu sends office traffic to the firewall first, which forwards it over the peering.
  - vpng-hub: rt-gateway-weu: spokes → 10.0.0.68
3. Out to the internet: Each spoke's route table sends 0.0.0.0/0 to the firewall, which takes it out with SNAT.
  - vmss-erp-prod: rt-erp-prod: 0.0.0.0/0 → 10.0.0.68
4. Spoke to spoke: Spokes are not peered to each other. ERP and intranet only meet through the firewall rules.
5. Admin access: No public IPs on VMs. Bastion reaches them over the peering; the NSG lets RDP in from Bastion only.
  - vmss-erp-prod: nsg-erp-prod: RDP from AzureBastionSubnet only

Layers
Ways to look at this drawing by tag.
- Routing: Route tables rt-erp-prod and rt-intranet-prod send 0.0.0.0/0 to the firewall; rt-gateway-weu sends the spoke ranges there too. (tags routing; 6 elements).
- Security: One way out to the internet, admin access only through Bastion. NSGs nsg-erp-prod and nsg-intranet-prod guard the spoke subnets. (tags security; 4 elements).
