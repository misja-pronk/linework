Production spoke

Azure infrastructure diagram: Northwind lakehouse · clusters with no public IP, storage over Private Link, the rest through afw-hub. 11 elements in 4 groups, with 9 connections.

  - Cluster VMs (Host and container subnets · SCC)
  - pep-dbw-lakehouse-prod (databricks_ui_api · back-end)
  - pep-stordersprod-dfs (dfs · snet-pep)
  - pep-dbstorage-prod-dfs (dfs · snet-pep)

Connections
- Cluster VMs connects to pep-dbw-lakehouse-prod (Private network: SCC relay · REST), connects to pep-stordersprod-dfs (Private network), connects to pep-dbstorage-prod-dfs (Private network) and connects to pep-dbstorage-prod-blob (Private network).
- pep-dbw-lakehouse-prod connects to dbw-lakehouse-prod (Private network: back-end).
- pep-stordersprod-dfs connects to stordersprod (Private network).
- pep-dbstorage-prod-dfs connects to dbstorage (Private network).
- pep-dbstorage-prod-blob connects to dbstorage (Private network).
- rt-lakehouse-prod connects to afw-hub (Private network: 0.0.0.0/0).

Walkthrough
1. Production spoke: Clusters run in the delegated host and container subnets, behind nsg-dbw-lakehouse-prod and rt-lakehouse-prod.
2. A cluster: Clusters dial out to the SCC relay and the REST API through the back-end endpoint; no inbound ports.
3. Reading data: Each domain's account has its own dfs endpoint, named in privatelink.dfs.core.windows.net.
4. Workspace storage: Firewall support closes dbstorage to public networks; clusters reach it through a dfs and a blob endpoint.
5. Everything else: rt-lakehouse-prod sends 0.0.0.0/0 to afw-hub and takes no gateway routes, so all else, on-premises too, passes the firewall.
