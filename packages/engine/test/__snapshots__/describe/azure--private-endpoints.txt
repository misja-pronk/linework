Private endpoints and DNS

Azure infrastructure diagram: Northwind · every PaaS service behind a private endpoint of its own, resolved the same way from Azure and from the office. 10 elements in 11 groups, with 9 connections.

Groups and elements
- Head office (10.10.0.0/16)
  - dc01.northwind.local (Conditional forwarders)
- sub-connectivity (Platform · Connectivity)
  - vnet-hub-weu (10.0.0.0/22)
    - snet-dns-in (10.0.1.0/28)
      - dnspr-hub (Inbound endpoint · 10.0.1.4)
    - snet-dns-out (10.0.1.16/28)
      - Outbound endpoint (Ruleset · northwind.local → 10.10.0.10)
  - rg-dns-privatelink (Zones linked to the hub)

Connections
- dc01.northwind.local connects to dnspr-hub.
- dnspr-hub connects to privatelink.database.windows.net, connects to privatelink.blob.core.windows.net and connects to Outbound endpoint.
- vmss-claims-prod connects to dnspr-hub, connects to pep-sql-claims-prod (Private network) and connects to pep-st-claims-prod (Private network).
- pep-sql-claims-prod connects to sql-claims-prod (Private network: TDS 1433).
- pep-st-claims-prod connects to stclaimsprod (Private network: HTTPS 443).

Walkthrough
1. Two services, one pattern: SQL and Storage are each reachable only through a private endpoint in the spoke.
2. The app asks for a name: The SQL name is a CNAME to its privatelink name; the resolver answers 10.1.9.4 from the private zone.
3. And connects privately: The connection goes to the endpoint's private IP in snet-pe, never over the internet.
4. From the office: Office DNS forwards database.windows.net and blob.core.windows.net to 10.0.1.4, so it gets the same private IPs.
  - dc01.northwind.local: database.windows.net → 10.0.1.4
5. Every service the same way: Key Vault, Service Bus… each gets an endpoint in snet-pe and a zone, linked to the hub once.
  - pep-sql-claims-prod: Next: pep-kv-claims-prod · 10.1.9.6
  - privatelink.database.windows.net: Next: privatelink.vaultcore.azure.net
