Azure landing zone

Azure infrastructure diagram: Northwind · management groups, subscriptions and the policy assigned at each level. 8 elements in 11 groups.

Groups and elements
- Tenant root group (northwind.onmicrosoft.com)
  - Northwind (Allowed regions · Defender for Cloud · diagnostics to Log Analytics)
    - Platform (Run by the platform team)
      - Management (Inherited policy only)
        - sub-management (Log Analytics · Automation)
      - Connectivity (DDoS protection on VNets)
        - sub-connectivity (Hub VNet · firewall · DNS)
      - Identity (No public IPs · VM backup)
        - sub-identity (Domain controllers)
    - Landing zones (Subnets need an NSG · no RDP/SSH from the internet · TLS 1.2)
      - Corp (No public IPs · PaaS only through private endpoints)
        - sub-erp-prod (Peered to the hub)
        - sub-intranet-prod (Peered to the hub)
      - Online (Own policy · WAF in front of web apps)
        - sub-shop-prod (Front Door · WAF)
    - Sandbox (No peering · no VPN or ExpressRoute)
      - sub-sandbox-dev (Experiments)
    - Decommissioned (No new resources)
      - sub-crm-old (Retired 2026)

Walkthrough
1. The hierarchy: Northwind's management groups, with a subscription per workload.
2. Policy is inherited: sub-erp-prod gets the policy of Northwind, Landing zones and Corp; each level below only adds to it.
3. Platform: Shared services in their own subscriptions, run by the platform team.
4. Landing zones: Corp workloads are private and peered to the hub; Online workloads face the internet behind a WAF.
5. Sandbox and decommissioned: Sandboxes cannot reach the corporate network; a retired subscription accepts no new resources.
